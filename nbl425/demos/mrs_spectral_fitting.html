Physical Principles & Mathematical Formulation

1. Chemical Shift Dispersion & Field Strength:

Electrons orbiting distinct chemical moieties generate local diamagnetic shielding currents that oppose the main field: B_{\text{local}} = B_0(1 - \sigma). Chemical shifts are reported on a dimensionless parts-per-million scale (δ = (\nu - \nu_{\text{ref}})/\nu_{\text{ref}} \times 10^6). While the chemical shift in ppm is field-independent, frequency dispersion in Hertz increases linearly with field strength (\Delta\nu_{\text{Hz}} = \gamma B_0 \Delta\delta). At 7.0 T, J-coupled multiplets (Glutamate, GABA) and crowded singlets (Cho, Cr) separate cleanly, reducing spectral covariance and fitting errors.

2. Basis Sets vs. Single-Peak Fitting:

In-vivo 1H spectra consist of overlapping scalar J-coupled multiplets and a broad macromolecular envelope. Rather than fitting unconstrained individual Gaussians, the Linear Combination Model constructs the total signal from quantum-mechanically simulated or phantom-recorded metabolite basis spectra: S(\delta) = \sum_k c_k B_k(\delta) \otimes L(\Delta\nu) + \text{Baseline}(\delta). This fixes relative peak multiplet areas, chemical shifts, and J-splitting within each molecule, leaving only overall concentration c_k, line broadening, and baseline to be estimated.

3. Cramer-Rao Lower Bounds (CRLB):

The Cramer-Rao inequality establishes the minimum possible variance achievable by any unbiased estimator: \text{Cov}(\hat{c}) \ge F^{-1} = \sigma^2 (X^T X)^{-1}, where X contains the metabolite basis columns and \sigma^2 is the noise variance. The relative uncertainty \text{CRLB} = (\text{SD}_k / \hat{c}_k) \times 100\% reflects not only noise, but severe covariance between overlapping metabolites. By convention in clinical spectroscopy, metabolite fits with \text{CRLB} > 20\% are classified as unreliable.

4. Diagnostic Neurochemical Markers:
  • NAA (2.02 ppm): Synthesized exclusively in neuronal mitochondria; marker of viable neuronal density. Drops during neurodegeneration, stroke, and neoplastic infiltration.
  • Creatine (3.03 ppm): Phosphocreatine energy reservoir; often serves as an internal concentration reference denominator.
  • Choline (3.22 ppm): Phosphocholine and glycerophosphocholine; elevated during cellular membrane synthesis and turnover (hallmark of active gliomas).
  • Lactate (1.33 ppm): Anaerobic glycolysis end-product; appears as an inverted/upright doublet in acute ischemia, mitochondrial disorders, and necrotic cysts.
