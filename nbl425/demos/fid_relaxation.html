Free Induction Decay (FID) & Spin Relaxation

Connecting the 3D physical evolution and relaxation of the nuclear magnetic moment — spin tipping, Larmor precession, isochromat dephasing, and $T_1$ recovery — directly to the voltage signal induced in an RF receiver coil via Faraday's Law of Induction.

Physical Principles:

1. Faraday's Law of Induction: An RF receiver coil positioned along the transverse x-axis detects the rate of change of magnetic flux: E(t) = -dΦ/dt ∝ -dMx(t)/dt. As the transverse magnetization vector Mxy rotates past the copper loop, it induces an alternating electrical voltage. When M points along the longitudinal z-axis (at equilibrium or after a 180° inversion pulse), Mxy = 0, producing zero flux change through the coil and no FID signal.

2. Microscopic Origin of the Decay (T2*): The net moment Mxy is the vector sum of billions of individual proton dipoles. Because local magnetic fields vary microscopically (ΔB0), individual spin isochromats precess at slightly different frequencies. Immediately after excitation they are in phase; over time they fan out (dephase) across the transverse plane. As their phase dispersion widens, their vector sum cancels out — producing the decaying exponential envelope of the Free Induction Decay.

3. T1 vs. T2* Asymmetry: In biological tissue, transverse dephasing (T2* ≈ 10–50 ms) happens orders of magnitude faster than longitudinal thermal recovery back to the lattice (T1 ≈ 500–1500 ms). Notice that long after the FID has vanished into noise, the longitudinal magnetization Mz is still slowly recovering toward +M0.
