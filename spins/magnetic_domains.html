What this figure is for

Nuclear spins are not the only magnetism in the scanner room. The electrons in the surrounding material matter just as much, and how they respond to B0 decides whether an object is harmless, an artifact, or a projectile. This is the lattice version of that story.

  • Ferromagnetic (T < Tc, the default). Neighbouring moments align with each other even without a field, forming domains. Sweep the field and you get a loop with area: the material remembers. Bring the field back to zero and magnetisation remains, which is remanence. Iron, nickel, cobalt, most steels. This is the projectile category, and the force does not switch off when the scan ends, because the magnet is always on.
  • Paramagnetic (raise T above 1.0). The domains dissolve and the loop collapses onto a single line through the origin: M follows H and forgets it immediately. Susceptibility is small and positive. Titanium, aluminium, gadolinium chelates, deoxyhaemoglobin. Safe to bring in, but still enough to distort the local field and cause susceptibility artifact.
  • Coercivity is the width of the loop. It is the reverse field needed to force the magnetisation back to zero. A wide loop is a hard magnet, a narrow one is soft. Lower the temperature and watch the loop widen.
  • Why this matters at the console. The field gradient at the bore entrance is what grabs a ferromagnetic object, so the danger peak is at the door, not at isocentre. And an implant labelled MR Conditional is one whose material sits in the second category, or whose geometry keeps the force and heating within limits.

Watch the domains coarsen. Straight after Randomise the lattice is a patchwork. Left alone, the domain walls cost energy, so the patches merge and the largest domain eats the rest until a single domain fills the lattice. Lower the temperature to freeze that process part-way; raise it toward 1.0 and the walls churn continuously.

Try this in class: set T/Tc to 0.6, press Sweep the field, and let one full loop draw. Then raise the temperature past 1.0 without clearing the plot. The fat loop and the thin line appear on the same axes, which is the clearest single picture of the difference between a ferromagnet and a paramagnet.

A note on the model: this is a two-dimensional Ising model with nearest-neighbour coupling, solved by Metropolis Monte Carlo. It is a cartoon of a real ferromagnet, but it produces genuine domains, a genuine Curie transition and a genuine hysteresis loop, all from one rule about neighbours.
