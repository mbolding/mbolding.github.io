Ensembles · coherence · the vector sum

Spin Ensembles & the Net Magnetisation

A lattice of magnetic moments, each precessing at its own Larmor frequency. The coil never sees any of them individually. It only sees the sum.

What this figure is for

An imaging voxel holds something like 1019 protons. This figure shows a handful of them, arranged on a lattice so you can see where each one sits in the sample, and asks the only question that matters for signal: do they agree on the phase?

  • Thermal equilibrium. Every moment precesses about B0, but the azimuths are random. The transverse parts cancel exactly and the sum lies along z. A stationary vector along B0 induces no voltage, which is why a magnet full of polarised protons is silent until you excite it.
  • Coherent. After a 90° pulse every moment points the same way in the transverse plane. Now the sum is as large as it can be and it rotates, so the coil sees a signal.
  • Spread of fields. Give each location a slightly different field and the phases fan out. The individual moments are undiminished, but their sum shrinks. Nothing has been lost yet, only scrambled, which is exactly why a 180° pulse can bring it back.

Turn on Shade spheres by field offset to see the field map itself: warm spheres sit in a higher field and run ahead, cool spheres lag. Then watch the phase colours sort themselves along that same gradient.

Try this in class: set the inhomogeneity to zero, apply a 90°, and note the flat signal. Now raise the inhomogeneity slider while it runs. The decay you create is T2*, and the dashed envelope shows how much slower the true T2 decay is underneath it.
