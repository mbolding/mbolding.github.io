Coherence · why the signal exists at all

Perfect Coherence

Every spin in the same field, every phase identical. The idealised case that real hardware spends its whole life trying to approximate.

What this figure is for

This is the reference case. The inhomogeneity slider starts at zero, so every location in the sample sees exactly the same B0, every moment precesses at exactly the same rate, and the phase fan never opens. The sum stays at full length and simply rotates.

  • Signal scales with coherence, not with the number of spins alone. Add spins with the lattice slider: the sum does not grow relative to its maximum, because it is an average. What grows in a real scanner is the absolute voltage, but only while the phases agree.
  • The only decay left is T2. With a uniform field there is nothing to refocus, so the signal falls at the true spin-spin rate and a 180° pulse buys you nothing. Drop T2 to 100 ms and watch the envelope tighten.
  • Now break it. Nudge the inhomogeneity slider off zero and the same demo becomes the dephasing demo. That single slider is the difference between T2 and T2*, and between a spin echo and a gradient echo.

Shim coils exist to push the inhomogeneity slider back toward zero over the volume you are imaging. A good shim is worth more signal than almost anything else you can do at the console.
