What this figure is for

The previous figures treat the sample as a single vector. This one insists on the fact that position matters: every cell sits in a slightly different magnetic field, so every cell accumulates phase at a different rate. The coil has no idea which cell is which. It receives one number, the vector sum, and that number collapses as soon as the cells disagree.

  • Watch the colours, not the arrows. Immediately after the 90° every cell is the same colour. As time passes the hue sweeps across the slice in step with the field map, which is exactly the phase ramp that a frequency-encoding gradient deliberately imposes during readout.
  • 180° versus reverse time. Reverse time is the cheat: it flips the sign of every precession rate, which no experiment can do. The 180° pulse achieves the identical refocusing by flipping the accumulated phase instead and letting the spins keep running forward at the same rates. Both bring the colours back together; only one of them is physics you can buy.
  • The blob is refocused too. Turn the susceptibility blob up to 30 Hz. Its cells race away from the rest and the signal dies almost at once, but a 180° still recovers the echo. This is why spin-echo EPI keeps signal near the sinuses where gradient-echo EPI drops out.
  • What is never recovered. Lower T2 to 200 ms and run the echo. The echo comes back on time but shorter, and it always lands on the dashed T2 curve. That curve is the ceiling: refocusing buys back the field-map dephasing and nothing else.

Try this in class: set τ to 500 ms, press Run a spin echo, and ask the room to predict where the echo peak will fall before it happens. Then change T2 and ask them to predict how tall it will be.
