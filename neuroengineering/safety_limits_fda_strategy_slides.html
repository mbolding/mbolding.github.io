Biophysical Exposure Limits

🧲 MRI / RF (SAR)

IEC 60601-2-33: Head SAR $\le 3.2\,\text{W/kg}$; Local 10-g tissue $\le 10\,\text{W/kg}$.

Prevents focal RF burns near conductive leads and eye cataracts.

🔊 Ultrasound (fUS)

IEC 60601-2-37: Mechanical Index $\text{MI} \le 1.9$; $I_{\text{spta}} \le 720\,\text{mW/cm}^2$.

In unossified neonatal fontanelles, conservative $\text{TIS} \le 0.7$ avoids focal cavitation.

💡 Optical / fNIRS

ANSI Z136.1 / IEC 60825: MPE $\le 0.2 - 0.4\,\text{W/cm}^2$.

Transducer skin contact temperature must remain $< 41^\circ\text{C}$ to prevent thermal injury.

The FDA CDRH Regulatory Pathway

Class I: General Controls

Low risk devices (e.g., standard passive surface electrodes).

Exempt from premarket notification; requires GMP registration and quality system (QSR).

Class II: 510(k) / De Novo

Moderate risk (diagnostic fNIRS, diagnostic ultrasound, clinical aEEG).

Demonstrates Substantial Equivalence (SE) to an existing predicate device within 90 days of review.

Class III: PMA

High risk / life-supporting (implantable DBS, intracortical BCI).

Requires prospective multi-center clinical trials proving safety and efficacy.

Grand Challenge Focus: Pediatric Device Laws

📜 Section 515A Pediatric Requirements

Mandates explicit pediatric subpopulation assessment (neonate, infant, child, adolescent).

Requires preclinical proof of skin safety on immature stratum corneum and long-term neurodevelopmental biocompatibility.

🌡️ Therapeutic Hypothermia Protocol Compliance

For HIE devices operating during 72-hr systemic cooling ($33.5^\circ\text{C}$):

Device must guarantee zero rewarming hot-spots ($< 0.5^\circ\text{C}$ local rise) to maintain neuroprotective hypothermic target temperatures.
