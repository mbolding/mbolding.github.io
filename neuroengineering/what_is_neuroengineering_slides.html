Seminar Opener

Seminar Opener · Discussion, not lecture

What Is Neuroengineering?

Seven definitions, eleven cases, and no agreement. An argument the field has not settled and this session will not settle either.

Press SPACE to advance · ESC for overview · S for facilitator notes
Total run time about 50 to 60 minutes with discussion. The deck is built to destabilize, not to resolve. Resist the urge to give an answer at the end. If someone demands one, say that the field's own flagship journal has not managed it either, and point at slide 13.

How this session works

  • You will write down a definition in the next two minutes, before you see anyone else's.
  • We will then run four cases at it. At least one will break it.
  • Only then do we look at the seven positions people actually hold, and at what the field has published about itself.
  • You leave with a better defended position, not a correct one. There is no answer slide.

The one rule

Attacking someone else's definition is easy and cheap. The useful move is naming what your own definition has to throw out, and saying whether you can live with it.

Set expectations hard here. Seminars on definitional questions fail when people think there is a right answer being withheld. Say plainly: there isn't one, and the interesting output is a defended position with known costs.

Do this now, before the next slide

Write one sentence: what makes something neuroengineering rather than neuroscience, biomedical engineering, or computer engineering?

Keep it. You will be asked to revise it at the end, and the revision is the point of the session.

2 minutes · silent · no phones
Enforce the silence and actually time it. The commitment device is what makes the later cases land: people defend what they have written down in a way they never defend a position they only heard. Do not let anyone start discussing yet.

If the room is small, collect a few verbatim definitions on a whiteboard now. You will refer back to them on slide 17.

The two answers almost everyone starts with

Applied neuroscience

Neuroscience discovers, engineering applies the findings.

Engineering for neuroscience problems

Engineering principles brought to bear on questions about the nervous system.

Show of hands. Is what you wrote a version of one of these?

Both are defensible. Both are about to have a hard four minutes.

Expect most hands up. That is the point: these two framings are the defaults, and the room should feel collectively invested in them before the cases start. Note who did NOT raise a hand and come back to them on slide 10, they probably wrote something close to Positions 4, 5 or 6.

Case 1 of 4

The cochlear implant

1960s to 1980s

Pursued over the objections of auditory scientists who had principled reasons to believe crude electrical stimulation could never carry speech. It went on to restore hearing to hundreds of thousands of people.

Does your definition let it in? If neuroengineering is applying neuroscience findings, which findings was it applying?

The honest complication: it did apply established tonotopy. What it defied was the view that fine temporal coding was required. So it applied the neuroscience that was right and ignored the part that was wrong, which you can only tell apart afterward.

So "engineering beat science" is too clean. Better framing: it applied the neuroscience that was right and ignored the part that was wrong, which you can only identify afterward.

Good question to push: if a device succeeds by ignoring the consensus, in what sense was the consensus being applied?

Case 2 of 4

The voltage clamp

1940s instrument, behind Hodgkin-Huxley

Hodgkin and Huxley could not have written their equations without it. The pattern repeats: the patch clamp created single-channel electrophysiology, BOLD contrast created human cognitive neuroscience.

The instrument came first. If engineering is downstream of neuroscience, why are the field's biggest gifts to neuroscience instruments?

The standing reply, offer it if the room gets too pleased with itself: instrument builders were steeped in the science of their day, and "application" need not mean applying a finished result. Cole and Marmont were applying an understanding of membrane behavior that existed, just not yet quantitatively. The pipeline story survives, it is just messier than the caricature.

Note this is the most divided row in the whole matrix: 2 in, 3 out, 2 arguable.

Case 3 of 4

A neuromorphic accelerator

event camera for robot vision, never touches tissue

Takes organizing principles from nervous systems and applies them to computation. Published in the field's journals, taught in its departments. No nervous system anywhere near it.

The arrow runs backwards. If your definition is about serving the nervous system, this is out, and you have excluded a chunk of the field's own literature.

Worth drawing out: the two positions that include it are the broadest reading and the purely sociological one. Nothing in between wants it. That is a strange shape and it should bother people.

Useful follow-up if there is time: a neuromorphic front end driving retinal stimulation may share most of its circuit design with this chip, and six of seven positions include that one. Should a definition separate two nearly identical circuits based on what they are wired to?

Case 4 of 4

Neuromarketing and neuroarchitecture

Both deploy real neuroscience toward practical ends: one to advertising, one to building design. Both have their own literature, conferences, and practitioners.

Neither is neuroengineering. But if "applying neuroscience to a practical end" is your criterion, on what grounds do you keep them out and the chip in?

The trap closing

Whatever admits the chip admits the ad agency. Same move, same direction, different destination.

This is the slide that does the real damage to the default answer, so give it room. If someone says "but neuroarchitecture isn't rigorous", press them: neuromarketing studies use the same instruments as cognitive neuroscience. Rigor is a quality question, not a membership question, and conflating them is its own error.

If anyone in the room has worked on neuroscience-informed design, ask them directly whether they consider that work neuroengineering. It usually splits the room and it is a genuinely open question, not a gotcha.

The squeeze

Read it narrowly

"Neuroscience findings applied toward biological ends."

Throws out the cochlear implant, the voltage clamp, and DBS. The founding achievements.

Read it broadly

"Neural principles applied toward any end."

Lets in neuromarketing, neurolaw, neuroeconomics, and neuroarchitecture. Stops naming a field.

There is no setting of the dial that keeps the founding cases and excludes the ad agency.

This is not a quibble about edge cases. It is one phrase carrying two incompatible meanings and borrowing whichever one survives the challenge.

Pause here. This is the pivot of the session. Ask directly: how many of you would now change what you wrote on slide 3? Get a count. Do not let people revise silently, the public admission is what makes the second half work.

Seven positions people actually hold

1Applied, broad

Neural principles toward any practical end.

2Applied, narrow

Neuroscience findings toward biological ends.

3Methods for science

Engineering method aimed at scientific questions.

4Interface discipline

Systems exchanging information or energy across the boundary.

5Clinical

Restoring, replacing, or augmenting function in an organism.

6Constraint

A living nervous system binds the design.

7Institutional

Whatever the field's institutions treat as its own.

Yours

Which of these is your slide-3 sentence closest to?

Do not editorialize about which is best. Each has a card on the reading page with its strongest case and its real cost. Three of them, positions 1, 2 and 6, have no published source at all and the page says so.

Ask for a rough hand count per position. Record it. It is worth comparing against where people land at the end.

What the field says about itself

Its own official definition is a conjunction

An emerging interdisciplinary research area that brings to bear neuroscience and engineering methods "to analyze neurological function as well as to design solutions to problems associated with neurological limitations and dysfunction." Journal of Neural Engineering editorial board, "What is Neural Engineering?" (2007)

That is Position 3 and Position 5 joined by an "as well as", with nothing said about how the two relate.

Written because the board judged the field had outgrown any clear statement of itself.

Worth stating plainly: the field convened its own editorial board to answer this question and produced a conjunction. That is not a failure of the board, it is evidence that the two purposes really are both present and really are not reducible to each other.

Sourcing caveat: publisher pages could not be opened directly when this page was built, so the wording should be checked against the primary text before anyone quotes it in writing.

And the record openly contradicts itself

The standard definition

Neuroengineering is a discipline within biomedical engineering using engineering techniques to understand, repair, replace, enhance, or otherwise exploit neural systems.

The founding conference

The first IEEE EMBS Neural Engineering conference, Capri, March 2003, was convened as a gathering for a field distinct from both neuroscience and biomedical engineering.

Both statements are in circulation. Nothing has resolved them.

Meanwhile the flagship journal's scope claims neuromorphic engineering, computational and systems neuroscience, and neuroimaging, wider than its own official definition covers.

This is the slide that answers "why hasn't someone just settled this." The field has tried, more than once, and the attempts do not agree with each other. A discipline can be perfectly healthy and productive while being unable to say what it is.

Analogy that lands well if you need one: nobody can define "life" to everyone's satisfaction either, and biology proceeds regardless.

What nobody disputes

The definitions differ. The constraints do not.

The target pushes back

Encapsulation begins in days. Day 400 is not the population you recorded on day 1.

The controller is learning too

The decoder adapts to the user while the user adapts to the decoder.

Observability is rationed

No non-invasive way to watch one human neuron. No budget buys out of the trade.

Harm is irreversible

One skull, one cochlea, one cord. The failure mode is not a product return.

The spec is often unknown

Nobody hands you the correct neural code. You co-discover it while building.

Timelines outrun careers

Cochlear implants: 1960s trials, mid-1980s approval. DBS: 1987 to 1997.

Put this in deliberately so the session does not end in nihilism. The definitional question is genuinely open; the practical character of the work is not. Students who find the first half frustrating usually settle here.

Why any of this matters

A definition is not decoration. Four things actually turn on it:

  • Curriculum. If neuroimaging is diagnostics and your definition is clinical, does it belong in the core?
  • Study section scope. Whether a proposal is reviewed by people who recognize the work, or by people who think it belongs somewhere else.
  • Hiring and promotion. Whether a candidate building neuromorphic hardware counts as a hire in the field or a hire adjacent to it.
  • What a student is told they are becoming. The one that matters most and gets discussed least.
Faculty in the room will have live examples of the middle two. Invite them. A concrete story about a proposal sent to the wrong panel does more than any argument on the earlier slides.

Break out · 15 minutes · groups of three or four

Pick one and argue it

  • Which case embarrasses your position most, and can you accept that exclusion?
  • An accelerator and a prosthesis front end may share most of their circuit design. Should a definition separate them?
  • Every position here excludes something the field's own institutions embrace. Defect in the definitions, or in the institutions?
  • Should a definition exclude bad work, or only misplaced work?
  • Does the field need a definition at all?
Assign questions rather than letting groups choose, otherwise everyone takes the last one because it is the easiest to be glib about. Question 1 is the most productive and should go to the group with the most confident people in it.

Reconvene with five minutes for each group to state a position and its cost. Enforce the cost half.

Close

Take out what you wrote at the start. What would you change, and which case made you change it?

If nothing changed, say which case you are willing to exclude to keep it. Either answer is a result. "It depends" is not.

3 minutes · then two or three out loud
Compare against the hand count you took on slide 10 if you took one. Movement toward Positions 4 through 7 is the common pattern; movement is not the goal, but noticing whether you moved is.

End without summarizing. The absence of a conclusion is the pedagogical point, and a tidy summary would undo the whole session.
